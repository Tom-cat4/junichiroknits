Chapter 1

Wait… This Was a Job?

In the summer of 2024, I was hired by RWTH Aachen University, one of Europe's top technical universities, to mentor 50 international students through the UROP 2024 research program. I organized 24 cultural excursions over 12 weeks, facilitated cross-cultural collaboration, and provided technical guidance throughout their projects.

I had done research at RWTH in 2023 and when they asked if anyone would return as a mentor… it took me about four seconds to say yes. Officially a job. Unofficially, the best summer of my life.

Chapter 3

Aachen, Home Base

Aachen is one of the most historically dense cities in Europe. Charlemagne made it the capital of his empire in the 800s, and that weight is everywhere: in the cathedral, the cobblestones, the buildings that somehow just still exist after 1,200 years. It sits right where Germany, Belgium, and the Netherlands meet, which makes it the perfect launchpad for everything else.

Aachen Cathedral, UNESCO World Heritage Site and Charlemagne's burial place.
Charlemagne. The man himself.

Chapter 6

Trier: Rome of Germany

Germany's oldest city. The Porta Nigra has been standing since the year 170 AD and just sits in the middle of town like it's nothing. Karl Marx was born here. Romans built baths here. The high cathedral has been consecrated since the 4th century. Trier makes everywhere else feel young.

Chapter 10

Dahenfeld: Family History

We made a detour. Gage's family roots trace back to a tiny German village called Dahenfeld. We drove there on a whim, found the street, found the records, and stood in front of the exact same house that exists in an old family photograph. History doesn't always feel this close.

The same house. Decades apart. Same cobblestones.

Chapter 12
🎂 Happy Birthday to Me

Berchtesgaden

The Bavarian Alps. A glacial lake with water so clear you can see the bottom from a boat. We hiked in, swam in water that came straight from a glacier (it absolutely did), and dried off on a mountain rock watching clouds move through the peaks.

It was my birthday. I genuinely don't know how to top this one.

Above right: the post-swim birthday selfie at Königssee. Those mountains are real. That water was freezing. Zero regrets.

Chapter 13

Hallstatt & Obertraun

One of the most photographed villages in the world, and you understand why the moment you see it. Perched on the edge of a mountain lake in the Austrian Alps, Hallstatt has been continuously inhabited for over 7,000 years, largely because of its salt mines. We stayed overnight for 12 euros. We did not want to leave.

Chapter 14

Rothenburg ob der Tauber

A medieval walled city that has barely changed since the 1600s. Cobblestone streets, half-timber houses, towers you can still walk up. The kind of place where you expect to turn a corner and see someone in a doublet. Incredible stop.

Chapter 15

Amsterdam

Last stop before the flight home. Canals, bikes, and a city that moves entirely at its own pace. Also: Gage had a very unexpected celebrity encounter while we were in the Netherlands.

Oh, One More Thing

While we were in the Netherlands, Gage got to meet Sally Pointer, experimental archaeologist, historical clothing maker, and YouTuber. She was dressed for the era she was recreating. Gage was in a t-shirt. Neither of them cared. Honorable mention of the trip.

Chapter 16

Flying Home Standby

We didn't have confirmed seats. We were flying standby. The most chaotic, most anxious, most triumphant way to end a summer in Europe. It worked out.

Epilogue

The Story Doesn't End There

The friendships you make while traveling have a way of following you home. Felix, who I first met in Aachen in 2023 and saw again throughout the whole summer of 2024, came to visit me in Michigan. Europe came to the Midwest.
